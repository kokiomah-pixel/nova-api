## Objective

Test whether operators need institution-owned review context that persists across agents, harnesses, wallets, risk systems, data providers, and execution environments before local authority acts.

The research must identify recurring workflow dependency rather than validate general interest in AI governance.

## Primary workflow qualification

Prefer a workflow with:

- a recurring consequential action;
- a named workflow owner;
- a named local authority;
- identifiable preparation and execution boundaries;
- accessible evidence sources;
- measurable review friction;
- observable outcome feedback; and
- willingness to refine the workflow.

## Research questions

### Governance continuity

- What review context is lost when a model, harness, provider, wallet, or execution system changes?
- Which prior decisions must remain interpretable after migration?
- Who owns the authoritative review record today?
- How much manual reconstruction is required before authority can decide?

### Action identity and review snapshots

- How does the institution distinguish a technical retry from a revised proposal or authority reconsideration?
- Which action and review-state versions must be preserved?
- What changes require a new review rather than reuse of prior context?

### External-system results

- Which policy, risk, wallet, compliance, or data-system results are consulted?
- Which results are authoritative, advisory, or disputed?
- How are disagreements between systems and local authority preserved?

### Machine spending

- Who owns the payer wallet and spending mandate?
- What context is required before signing or incurring expense?
- How is paid-data provenance preserved when the output later influences a capital review?
- Which purchase events are decision-significant enough to retain?

### Discovery and private invocation

- Is public capability discovery useful?
- Must invocation remain private, authenticated, tenant-scoped, and workflow-authorized?
- Which identity, confidentiality, and retention controls are required?
- Does payment create access only, or is a separate institutional authorization path required?

### Chronology reuse and portability

- Has a prior review changed how the next comparable action was handled?
- What removal cost appears when chronology is unavailable?
- Would the institution pay for portability across provider migration?
- When does flat chronology become insufficient for causal replay, disagreement history, or similar-case retrieval?

## Decision triggers

Escalate for CCO review when evidence repeats that:

- no action is considered review-ready without reconstructed cross-system context;
- operators need model- or provider-independent authority history;
- prior chronology materially changes later review;
- private invocation with public discovery is repeatedly requested;
- paid-data provenance becomes a recurring institutional requirement;
- provider migration creates material loss of governance meaning; or
- operators identify a budget and recurring commercial unit.

## Non-claims

Research participation, interest, or positive feedback does not establish adoption, buyer pull, pricing power, product-market fit, institutional dependency, or a moat.
