# Circle Agent Marketplace and Agent-Ready API Distribution Signal

## Executive determination

Circle is encouraging API providers to expose services that autonomous agents
can discover, evaluate, invoke, pay for in USDC, and consume
programmatically.

This is accepted as evidence that Circle is developing a credible machine
service-discovery and API-distribution channel.

The evidence does not establish buyer demand for Sharpe Nova OS,
institutional permission to expose Nova publicly, suitability of anonymous
pay-per-call access, product-market fit, pricing power, or authorization for a
Circle-specific implementation.

The approved Nova response is documentation, architecture refinement,
operator research, marketplace preflight, commercial-model research, and
market monitoring.

No public application, listing, runtime integration, x402 activation, retail
service, or accepted product-state change is authorized.

## Source provenance reconciliation

The Market-Signal Extraction record supplied the source label
“Turn Your API Into an Agent-Ready Revenue Stream.”

First-party verification identified Circle's May 18, 2026 article,
“Turn Your API into a Storefront for Agents,” as the canonical matching source
for the described Agent Marketplace, USDC payment, x402, service-discovery, and
API-monetization claims.

The supplied label and the verified first-party title are preserved separately
because they are not exact title matches.

Circle's live Agent Marketplace and Circle for Agents surfaces are recorded as
supporting sources. They corroborate the current marketplace and payment
framing but do not independently establish institutional demand for Nova,
private marketplace suitability, access isolation, or implementation authority.

## Evidence and inference boundary

### Observed market evidence

Circle is promoting an agent-ready API model in which autonomous agents may:

```text
discover
→ evaluate
→ call
→ pay
→ consume
```

Circle identifies possible service categories including data, intelligence,
financial analysis, market information, risk signals, compliance services,
infrastructure, and real-world execution.

### Approved inference

Agent marketplaces may become meaningful distribution surfaces for
machine-callable services.

Nova may eventually benefit from being machine-discoverable through one or
more such channels.

### Unvalidated hypotheses

The evidence does not establish that:

- institutions want to discover Nova through Circle;
- Circle will support the access isolation Nova requires;
- a marketplace listing will create qualified institutional demand;
- institutional buyers want marketplace-based metering;
- retail agent usage is commercially desirable;
- public discovery and private invocation can be supported without category
  dilution;
- Circle should become a preferred or exclusive channel.

Circle is an example channel, not a Nova dependency. Distribution must remain
nonexclusive.

## What the evidence establishes

- Circle is presenting agent discovery, evaluation, invocation, payment, and
  consumption as a connected distribution model.
- Machine-readable service catalogs and USDC payment are becoming credible
  distribution surfaces for agent-callable APIs.
- Providers are being encouraged to package stable service descriptions,
  invocation behavior, and commercial terms for machine customers.
- Distribution infrastructure can compress discovery and consumption without
  providing institutional identity, permission, or source authority.
- Circle's canonical seller article describes Agent Marketplace as a surface
  where services can be discovered, evaluated, and paid for by agents.
- Circle describes Gateway as supporting x402 payment flows for USDC
  nanopayments.
- Circle's live marketplace presents machine-callable services and paid API
  access as an active distribution model.
- Circle's public agent surface uses “Payment as authentication” as an
  agent-resource-access framing.

## What the evidence does not establish

- Buyer demand, product-market fit, pricing power, or a Nova retail use case.
- That a public listing is safe or desirable for Nova.
- That marketplace, wallet, or payment identity establishes an institution.
- That Circle supports private, tenant-scoped, workflow-authorized invocation.
- That marketplace payment may expose institution-owned data or chronology.
- That `/v1/context` should become public, retail, or marketplace-facing.
- That Circle should own Nova's identity, chronology, pricing, or route to
  market.
- The Circle phrase “Payment as authentication” does not establish that payment
  is sufficient for Nova institutional identity, enterprise tenancy, workflow
  authorization, or institution-owned data access.
- The public marketplace does not establish that Circle supports private,
  unlisted, organization-scoped, tenant-isolated, or contract-backed Nova
  invocation.
- The first-party materials do not establish buyer demand for Nova.
- The first-party materials do not authorize a Circle application, listing,
  manifest, retail service, x402 integration, or runtime change.

## Circle payment-authentication framing

Circle's public agent surface uses the phrase:

> Payment as authentication.

This describes Circle's commercial resource-access framing.

Sharpe Nova OS does not adopt that equivalence for institution-specific access.

For Nova, payment may satisfy an external commercial access condition. It
cannot establish:

- institutional identity;
- enterprise tenancy;
- workflow authorization;
- action-class permission;
- institution-owned data entitlement;
- access to institution-specific constraints;
- access to authority maps;
- access to private evidence sources;
- access to institutional chronology;
- access to institution-governed Reflex Memory;
- source authority;
- review completeness;
- local decision authority;
- capital-action approval.

```text
Payment accepted
≠ institution authenticated
≠ tenant authorized
≠ private context accessible
≠ authority established
```

This distinction reinforces the approved clean break between public discovery,
retail agent usage, and private institutional review context.

## Architect-approved access-class amendment

The original public-discovery/private-invocation distinction is necessary but
not sufficient.

Nova must maintain three separate access classes:

1. public capability discovery;
2. optional future retail agent usage;
3. private institutional review context.

Retail-accessible usage must not share an institution-owned data plane.

A retail user cannot become an institutional tenant through payment,
subscription upgrade, wallet ownership, API-key tier, endpoint reuse, or
metadata submission.

## Public discovery implications

Public discovery could eventually expose boundary-safe, non-callable
capability metadata and direct qualified institutions to onboarding. It must
not expose tenant identities, institution-specific constraints, authority
maps, private sources, chronology identifiers, connector details, pricing, or
credentials. No discovery surface or manifest is authorized.

## Retail agent implications

A future retail service would require a separate business case, approval,
service identity, endpoint, credentials, data plane, source registry, cache,
retention policy, chronology, and Reflex Memory boundary. It could use only
public, synthetic, or explicitly supplied non-institutional information.

Retail usage cannot upgrade into enterprise tenancy through payment,
subscription, wallet ownership, metadata, credential tier, or silent routing.
No retail service is authorized.

## Institutional review-context implications

Institutional Nova remains private, authenticated, tenant-scoped,
workflow-authorized, action-class restricted, and contract-and-policy governed.
Classification and authorization must occur before institution-owned sources,
configuration, chronology, or Reflex Memory are accessed.

Agent prepares action. Nova structures review context. Local authority decides.
External systems execute. Nova does not execute.

## Data-plane and chronology separation

Retail and institutional services must not share invocation endpoints,
credentials, tenant identities, data stores, vector stores, caches, source
registries, encryption keys, logs, retention policies, chronology namespaces,
Reflex Memory namespaces, training corpora, or billing entitlements.

Retail activity does not enter institutional chronology or influence
institutional Reflex Memory automatically. Cross-plane migration requires
recognized institutional identity, explicit authorization, provenance and
classification review, and separate governed acceptance.

## Authentication, payment, and authority separation

Payment may establish that an external commercial requirement was satisfied.
It does not authenticate an institution, bind a tenant, authorize a workflow,
grant source access, complete review context, or create authority. A successful
payment with absent institutional permission must result in no
institution-specific invocation.

## Endpoint implications

`/v1/context` remains a private authenticated Legacy v1 surface. It is not a
retail or marketplace endpoint and is not represented as the approved future
external contract. A future retail surface would require a separate service
identity and endpoint review. No Circle-specific route is authorized.

## Marketplace preflight implications

The internal
[`Circle Agent Marketplace Preflight`](../../../go-to-market/circle-agent-marketplace-preflight.md)
must verify the exact program, source, access model, identity, confidentiality,
ownership, chronology, metering, nonexclusivity, and listing requirements
before any later application recommendation. Completion does not authorize a
submission.

## Operator-research implications

Research must test whether operators use service catalogs, require public
discovery with private invocation, insist on retail/institutional isolation,
accept marketplace metering after enterprise agreement, and require portable
institution-owned chronology. Answers remain research evidence, not listing,
runtime, pricing, or product authority.

## Commercial-model implications

```text
Retail commercial object:
A separately approved bounded service using public, synthetic,
or user-supplied non-institutional information.

Institutional commercial object:
A private configured review environment covering recurring
institutionally governed action classes, approved sources,
constraint applicability, authority handoff, and institution-owned chronology.
```

Enterprise license, enterprise license plus usage, private machine invocation,
institution-scoped metering, and a nonexclusive marketplace discovery role are
approved for research only. Anonymous public context sales are rejected for
now. No price, forecast, or willingness-to-pay claim is accepted.

## Competitive and category implications

Circle may provide distribution, payment, or wallet infrastructure. Nova must
preserve provider neutrality and its independent institutional context,
source-authority representation, unresolved conditions, authority handoff, and
institution-owned chronology. A marketplace must not capture Nova's category
or become its exclusive channel.

## Approved actions

- Preserve this Circle distribution signal as market evidence.
- Create the provider-neutral three-access-class specification.
- Complete an internal research preflight and listing draft.
- Extend operator research and market monitoring.
- Research private and unlisted distribution, enterprise identity,
  confidentiality, contract-backed metering, and nonexclusive channels.
- Cross-link machine spending to the retail/institutional separation rule.

## Risk assessment

| Risk | Required treatment |
|---|---|
| Nova becomes a commodity intelligence API | Keep institutional service private and workflow-scoped |
| Retail pricing anchors institutional value downward | Separate commercial models and service classes |
| Marketplace payment is mistaken for permission | Preserve payment, authentication, tenancy, workflow authorization, and local authority as separate objects |
| Retail access reaches institution-owned information | Require hard data-plane isolation |
| `/v1/context` becomes a mixed retail and institutional endpoint | Reserve it for private institutional use |
| Retail usage contaminates institutional chronology | Prohibit automatic cross-plane chronology |
| Institutional data improves retail outputs without permission | Prohibit cross-plane training and retrieval by default |
| Circle captures Nova's category | Preserve provider neutrality and nonexclusive distribution |
| Public listing exposes confidential metadata | Limit discovery to boundary-safe capability metadata |
| Market direction is mistaken for buyer demand | Require operator validation before listing or build |

## Final disposition

Architect and Jarvis-Nova CCO review accept the market evidence, provider-neutral
access-class architecture, internal preflight, operator research, commercial
research, and monitoring actions at specification or research level only.

Circle remains an example, nonexclusive channel. Buyer demand, operator need,
listing suitability, retail-service need, product-market fit, and pricing
power remain unvalidated. No runtime, production, listing, manifest, retail,
x402, chronology, or Reflex Memory change is authorized.

## Category protection

Nova is not a generic risk API, retail investment-recommendation API, public
compliance check, public transaction-clearance service, marketplace
authorization service, x402 entitlement service, wallet-controlled governance
service, Circle-native governance product, or marketplace-owned institutional
context layer.

```text
Marketplace discovery may identify the capability.

Retail usage, if separately authorized, remains non-institutional.

Institutional Nova remains private, tenant-scoped decision-context
infrastructure before local authority acts.
```

> Circle may provide a marketplace, machine customer, wallet, payment rail, or
> discovery surface. Nova must preserve institutional context, source
> authority, constraint applicability, unresolved conditions, local-authority
> handoff, and institution-owned chronology independently of Circle.
