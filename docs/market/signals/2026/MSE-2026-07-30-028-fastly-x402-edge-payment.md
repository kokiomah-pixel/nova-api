# Executive determination

Fastly demonstrated that programmable edge infrastructure can require an
x402 payment, return a payment challenge, verify a machine-supplied payment,
and release a protected resource without forwarding unpaid traffic to the
origin application.

This is accepted as evidence that the path between machine intent, economic
commitment, and resource delivery is compressing into ordinary internet
infrastructure.

The evidence does not establish institutional permission, source authority,
review completeness, buyer demand for Sharpe Nova OS, or authorization for a
Nova x402 implementation.

The approved Nova response is specification refinement, operator research,
and market monitoring—not payment-system development.

## Evidence classification

The reviewed evidence is Fastly's first-party [Automated Payments at the Edge
with x402 and Fastly Compute](https://www.fastly.com/blog/automated-payments-at-the-edge-with-x402-and-fastly-compute).
It is a working infrastructure demonstration using test wallets and testnet
USDC. Fastly explicitly presents it as a proof of edge enforcement rather than
a production payment architecture.

Evidence and inference are separated in this record:

- **Observed evidence:** a protected route returned an x402 challenge; a
  configured external payer signed the challenge; edge middleware verified the
  supplied payment; verified traffic received the resource; unpaid traffic did
  not.
- **Approved inference:** machine discovery, payment challenge, payment
  verification, and resource delivery can be compressed into programmable
  internet infrastructure.
- **Unvalidated hypothesis:** institutions may need durable review context
  before machine expenditure and when purchased output later supports a
  consequential capital review.

Fastly is an example provider. It is not a Nova dependency or an authority for
institutional review.

## What the evidence establishes

- Edge or gateway infrastructure can present a machine-readable payment
  requirement before allowing access to a protected resource.
- An external wallet and payment flow can satisfy that requirement.
- The enforcement layer can verify supplied payment evidence and fail closed.
- Resource access can occur without the origin application handling unpaid
  traffic.
- The interval between machine request and resource delivery can be short and
  automated.

## What the evidence does not establish

- That a cryptographically valid payment was institutionally permitted.
- That the payer, provider, resource, price, terms, or jurisdiction were
  approved.
- That purchased output is authoritative or fit for evidentiary reliance.
- That review context is complete or a capital action is approved.
- That operators have a recurring problem, an identified budget, or
  willingness to pay for Nova.
- That Nova should build, expose, or activate x402, wallet, signing,
  verification, settlement, or resource-release capability.

## Central strategic distinction

```text
x402 and edge enforcement:
Was the required payment validly supplied?

Sharpe Nova OS:
What institution-defined context surrounded the proposed machine expenditure,
and how should the resulting resource be treated if it later influences a
consequential capital review?
```

```text
Payment verified
≠ institutionally permitted
≠ provider approved
≠ source authoritative
≠ review context complete
≠ capital action approved
```

Agent prepares action. Nova structures review context. Local authority decides.
External systems execute. Nova does not execute.

## Architectural consequences

At specification level, machine spending requires distinct representations for
resource discovery or quotation, resource acquisition, and evidentiary
reliance. Spending governance and information governance must remain separate.
External payment results may be observed as provider-attributed facts but have
no institutional, evidentiary, or capital-action authority effect.

The durable specification is
[`docs/architecture/machine-spending-review-context.md`](../../../architecture/machine-spending-review-context.md).
No product requirement or public interface follows from this record.

## Chronology implications

Preserve decision significance, not every machine transaction. Discovery is not
retained by default. Acquisition is retained only when governance-material.
Evidentiary reliance may become a candidate for governed retention when it
materially influences consequential review.

This market signal is not an institutional action, operator decision, reviewed
purchase, or observed Nova outcome. It creates no chronology candidate,
accepted event, ledger change, or automatic ingestion behavior.

## Reflex Memory implications

A future, separately authorized system might learn from reviewed patterns such
as repeated usefulness, rejection, duplication, lateness, or conflicts
associated with paid sources. Those patterns would require governed evidence
and formal institutional acceptance.

This evidence record creates no Reflex Memory object. It does not approve a
provider, alter a mandate, create purchase authority, increase spending limits,
or authorize a wallet signature.

## Operator-research implications

The unvalidated institutional question is whether operators need structured
context before an external wallet signs and whether they need provenance when
purchased output later supports a claim. Research must test recurrence,
materiality, ownership, budget, current failure modes, and cross-provider
continuity. Market commentary alone is not buyer-pull evidence.

The canonical operator-discovery template contains the approved questions at
[`docs/go-to-market/treasury-operator-discovery-template.md`](../../../go-to-market/treasury-operator-discovery-template.md).

## Discoverability, invocation, payment, and authority separation

```text
Discoverable
≠ callable
≠ institutionally permitted
≠ economically authorized
≠ paid
≠ authoritative
≠ review complete
≠ capital-action approval
```

A service being discoverable or callable does not grant institutional
permission. Payment does not grant evidentiary authority. Resource access does
not make a consequential action ready for review or approved for execution.

## Approved actions

- Record this market evidence and its provenance.
- Define machine-spending review context at specification level.
- Separate spending governance from information governance.
- Add operator-research questions without converting them into requirements.
- Monitor edge payment, wallets, facilitators, gateways, paid services, and
  machine discovery across providers.
- Preserve institution-owned context through provider changes.
- Classify edge-payment outputs as non-authoritative external observations.

## Required CCO refinements

- Treat resource discovery, acquisition, and evidentiary reliance as three
  distinct events.
- Make chronology selective and reference-first.
- Record nonfinancial consequences such as contract, confidentiality,
  jurisdiction, capability, and dependency effects.
- Keep purchase permission separate from the treatment of purchased output.
- Validate operator need before build, GTM positioning, or monetization.
- Preserve portability across wallets, edge providers, facilitators, and
  resource providers.

## Actions not authorized

- Public x402 registration, discovery, pricing, or activation.
- A payment-gated context endpoint or any new x402 route.
- Nova-managed wallets, signing, verification, settlement, payment policy, or
  spending approval.
- Automatic chronology ingestion or retention of every machine purchase.
- Automatic Reflex Memory mutation or provider approval.
- Treating payment success as review readiness or capital authority.
- Runtime, API, infrastructure, deployment, credential, or production changes.

## Category protection

Nova is not a payment gateway, x402 facilitator, wallet-policy engine,
spending-control product, edge-enforcement service, signing service, settlement
service, transaction-authorization system, resource-access authority, or
paid-data marketplace.

> x402 and edge infrastructure determine whether a payment requirement was
> satisfied and whether a resource may be served. Sharpe Nova OS structures the
> institution-defined review context surrounding why a machine expenditure was
> proposed and how the resulting resource should be treated if it later
> influences a consequential capital review.

```text
Nova structures.
Institutional authority interprets.
External wallets and payment systems act.
```

## Risk assessment

| Risk | Current treatment |
|---|---|
| Payment validity is mistaken for institutional permission | Preserve explicit non-equivalence |
| Purchased output is treated as authoritative | Separate spending and information governance |
| Nova drifts into payment or execution authority | Prohibit runtime and payment-system work |
| Every low-value transaction is retained | Apply selective, reference-first chronology |
| Provider-specific coupling weakens continuity | Use portable references and provider attribution |
| Market direction is mistaken for buyer demand | Keep operator hypothesis unvalidated |
| Low price hides material nonfinancial consequences | Record contract, data, jurisdiction, capability, and dependency effects |

## Escalation triggers

- Pre-signing review requirements repeat across operators.
- Provider-specific machine-spending mandates repeat.
- Paid-source provenance becomes an institutional requirement.
- Operators cannot reconstruct why agent purchases occurred.
- Purchased outputs materially influence capital reviews.
- Payment and evidence provenance converge.
- Edge or wallet vendors claim complete agent governance.
- Context is lost when a wallet, facilitator, edge, or resource provider
  changes.
- Financial operators request pre-signing context.

Escalation permits review, not implementation. A separate Architect and CCO
decision remains required.
